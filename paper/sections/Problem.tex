\section{Problem definition}\label{sec:problem}
A water distribution network is an undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ whose vertices are junctions with coordinates $p_v\in\mathbb{R}^2$, a subset $S\subseteq V$ of which are water sources, and whose edges are pipes with a capacity $c(u,v)>0$. The length of a pipe is $\ell(u,v)=\lVert p_u-p_v\rVert_2$, and $d_G(u,v)$ denotes the shortest-path distance through the network under $\ell$. The Water Graph Problem \citep{narvaez_slides} poses seven questions on this model. This report covers all seven as a single study, because their results feed each other: lengths define distances, distances define sectors, and sectors define the flow problems. The seven computational problems, with their input and expected output, are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textbf{T1}] \textbf{Representation.} Build an adjacency list and an adjacency matrix of $G$, explore $G$ with BFS and DFS, find its connected components, and compare the two representations. \emph{Input:} $V$ and $E$. \emph{Output:} both representations, the traversal orders, the list of components, and the measured time and memory of each representation.
    \item[\textbf{T2}] \textbf{Pipe lengths.} Compute every pipe length and compare straight-line with network distance. \emph{Input:} the coordinates and the pipes. \emph{Output:} $\ell(u,v)$ for every pipe, the shortest, longest and total length, the ratio $d_G(u,v)/\lVert p_u-p_v\rVert$ for every pair of nodes, and a structure that answers length queries efficiently.
    \item[\textbf{T3}] \textbf{Sectorization.} Assign every node to its closest source, $\sigma(v)=\arg\min_{s\in S}d_G(s,v)$, and find the pipes to close, $C=\{(u,v)\in E:\sigma(u)\neq\sigma(v)\}$. \emph{Input:} $G$, $\ell$ and $S$. \emph{Output:} the nodes of each sector $V_s=\{v:\sigma(v)=s\}$, the set $C$, and a colour-coded map.
    \item[\textbf{T4}] \textbf{Freshness.} Water age is taken as proportional to $d_G$; find the node of each sector that receives the oldest water, $t_s=\arg\max_{v\in V_s}d_G(s,v)$. \emph{Input:} the sectors and distances of T3. \emph{Output:} $t_s$, $d_G(s,t_s)$ and the route from $s$ to $t_s$ for every sector, and the comparison of distances before and after sectorization.
    \item[\textbf{T5}] \textbf{Maximum flow.} Find the largest flow from $s$ to $t_s$ inside each sector such that $|f(u,v)|\le c(u,v)$ on every pipe and flow is conserved at every other node. \emph{Input:} the open pipes of each sector, their capacities, $s$ and $t_s$ (T4). \emph{Output:} the maximum flow value, the flow on each pipe and the bottleneck pipes (a minimum cut).
    \item[\textbf{T6}] \textbf{Sampling route.} Find a minimum-length closed walk on $G$ that starts and ends at the node labelled office and visits every node; nodes and pipes may be repeated, since $G$ need not contain a Hamiltonian cycle. \emph{Input:} $G$, $\ell$ and the office node. \emph{Output:} a closed walk over real pipes, its length, and whether it is proven optimal.
    \item[\textbf{T7}] \textbf{Expansion.} Link a new point $q$ by one pipe to its nearest non-source node, $\arg\min_{v\in V\setminus S}\lVert p_v-q\rVert$, and update the representations. \emph{Input:} $G$ and the coordinates and capacity of each new point. \emph{Output:} the selected node and the length of the new pipe, the updated network, and the cost of the search and of the update.
\end{enumerate}
